Can the College Library keep its services whole on a flat FAS subvention?

A five-year financial plan for Harvard College Library, FY27 to FY31, built from published FAS and Harvard Library figures and my own planning model. Narissara Khomkhai, November 2026.

Not on its current path. Yes, in three steps. In my model the unrestricted budget runs −$1.3M in FY27, and the gap grows to −$10.5M by FY31 without action. Six levers worth $6.3M a year hold the reserve above its $5M floor through FY29. A second stage and a structural decision, both taken in the FY29 budget, bring the unrestricted budget to $0.0M in FY31 with the reserve back at $10.0M.

Why now

FAS unrestricted funds fell from $207M to $112M in three years F. Harvard held the FY27 endowment payout flat to absorb the 8% excise tax F. The College Library lives on both.

What the model shows

The consolidated result looks balanced at $0.2M. Take out the $1.5M of restricted income left unspent and the unrestricted result is −$1.3M, drawn from the reserve P.

What I would do first

Report unrestricted and restricted results apart, review every restricted fund with Accounting, and agree a serials renewal strategy with Collections before the January quotes.

The problem, in one line

55% of revenue is a FAS subvention growing 0% to 1%, and 29% is restricted by donor terms. Costs grow 3.9% a year. Without action the unrestricted gap reaches −$10.5M by FY31.

FP&A keeps the unrestricted result in view, moves restricted money to every cost it can lawfully cover, and puts choices in front of leaders before the gap decides for them.

Can we close the gap and protect the mission?

Yes, in three stages. Stages 1 and 2 keep core services whole (study space, core collections and term-time access) while trimming low-use hours. Stage 3 changes how some services are delivered, so it is a leadership decision with its own guard. Every lever has a size you can trace, a trigger, an owner and a mission guard.

Stage 3: the structural decision

Taken in the FY29 budget, in effect from FY30 P

$3.0M a year

About 3% of costs. Options to cost by the FY29 budget: where and how services are delivered, technical services workflow, and the subvention rule with FAS.

Guard: student study space, core research collections and term-time access hours stay whole. Any option that touches them goes to the Vice President for the Harvard Library as a trade-off, not a saving.

What must be true

  • Fund stewards confirm that $1.5M a year of current costs meet donor terms.
  • Collections holds renewals near 5% through package terms and usage-based cancellations.
  • The twelve vacant positions stay open through attrition without cutting public service hours.
  • FAS keeps the subvention at least flat for FY28, as in its FY26 guidance.
  • Library leadership and FAS take the structural decision in the FY29 budget, not later.

Testing generative AI before we spend

Five tests every proposal must pass

  1. 1Use case A task with volume and a clear right answer: draft metadata, invoice matching, fund coding.
  2. 2Accuracy Tested on a sample against staff work. The pilot proceeds only above an agreed accuracy rate.
  3. 3People review A person reviews every record before it is published or paid.
  4. 4Cost and benefit License, setup and review time against hours saved, shown as a business case with NPV.
  5. 5Data and licensing No licensed content or personal data leaves Harvard-approved tools.

Who does what, by when?

The first 90 days follow the FAS planning calendar. The team runs on one calendar, one set of controls and a backup for every task.

Days 1 to 30 · Learn

  • Meet each analyst, budget manager and fund steward
  • Learn the team’s tools: HUBS, SmartView, Oracle and the capital systems
  • Rebuild the Q2 forecast fund by fund

Days 31 to 60 · Build

  • Restricted fund review with Accounting
  • Renewal strategy with Collections
  • FY28 budget and multi-year financial plan (MYFP), three scenarios, three stages

Days 61 to 90 · Deliver

  • FY28 plan to FAS with the MYFP and capital view
  • Dashboard, trigger board and a quarterly leadership deck
  • Cross-training matrix and written procedures in place

How I would run the FP&A team

A collaborative, service-first team that owns its quality: supervision, development, workload, service, controls and continuity

Lead and develop

  • Weekly one-to-ones and a development goal for each person
  • A 30-day onboarding plan: HUBS, SmartView, the close and the forecast
  • One stretch assignment each cycle, such as owning the collections forecast

Workload and priorities

  • One planning calendar: close on day 8, forecast on day 15
  • Priorities set each Monday, workloads balanced across the team
  • I take overflow in peak weeks: budget build and year-end

Service and improvement

  • Response times agreed with budget managers
  • A short review after each close and forecast: what to fix next time
  • One process improvement or automation each cycle, tested before it ships

Controls and documentation

  • A preparer and a reviewer on every work product
  • Tie-out to the ledger before anything leaves the team
  • A shared file structure with naming standards and a written procedure for every recurring task

Hiring, succession and continuity

  • Structured interviews with a short modeling exercise
  • A primary and a backup for every process, so the close survives any absence
  • A succession note for each role, reviewed once a year

Salary growth and the FAS subvention move the result most, so they get the closest watch and the earliest conversations with HR and FAS Finance.

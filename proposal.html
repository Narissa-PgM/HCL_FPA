Can the College Library keep its services whole on a flat FAS subvention?

A five-year financial plan for Harvard College Library, FY27 to FY31, built from published FAS and Harvard Library figures and my own planning model. Narissara Khomkhai, November 2026.

Yes, through FY30. Costs grow about 4% a year while most revenue grows 0% to 1%. Without action the yearly gap reaches −$8.2M by FY31. Six levers worth $6.8M a year close it through FY30 without cutting service hours or core collections. FY31 still shows −$1.4M, so the FY29 budget must settle the service model with FAS.

Why now

FAS unrestricted funds fell from $207M to $112M in three years F. Harvard held the FY27 endowment payout flat to absorb the 8% excise tax F. The College Library lives on both.

What I found

The FY27 forecast balances at $0.2M only because twelve positions sit vacant P. Meanwhile $1.5M of restricted income goes unspent each year P, the same pattern FAS reports at scale.

What I would do first

Review every restricted fund, rebuild the Q2 forecast fund by fund, and agree a serials renewal strategy with Collections before the January quotes land.

The problem, in one line

55% of revenue is a FAS subvention growing 0% to 1%, and 29% is restricted by donor terms. Costs grow about 4% a year. Without action the yearly gap reaches −$8.2M by FY31.

The job of FP&A here is to keep that gap visible early, move restricted money to every cost it can lawfully cover, and give leaders choices before the gap chooses for them.

Reading the fan

The median path ends FY31 at $8.6M. One path in ten ends below −$8.2M, one in ten above $25.6M.

38% of paths cross the $5M floor at least once by FY31. That share is the case for triggers: the plan does not wait for the annual budget to act.

Six levers$6.8M

What must be true

  • Fund stewards confirm that at least $1.8M a year of current costs meet donor terms.
  • Collections can hold renewals near 5% by package terms and usage-based cancellations.
  • Normal attrition, about 8% of 410 staff a year, gives room to leave 15 roles unfilled over three years.
  • FAS keeps the subvention at least flat for FY28, as in its FY26 guidance.
  • The AI metadata pilot passes its accuracy test before any role changes.

Second-stage levers, held in reserve

Used only when a trigger fires, as in the downside case P

  • About $1.0M a year: move non-urgent building projects back a year, with FAS Facilities.
  • About $0.8M a year: reduce print and duplicate purchasing where an e-version is licensed.
  • About $0.4M a year: summer hours at lower-use sites, with study space kept open at Lamont and Widener.
  • Timing, not cuts: ask FAS for a multi-year subvention bridge, backed by the trigger history.

Testing generative AI before we spend

Five tests every proposal must pass

  1. 1Use case A task with volume and a clear right answer: draft metadata, invoice matching, fund coding.
  2. 2Accuracy Tested on a sample against staff work. The pilot proceeds only above an agreed accuracy rate.
  3. 3People review A person reviews every record before it is published or paid.
  4. 4Cost and benefit License, setup and review time against hours saved, shown as a business case with NPV.
  5. 5Data and licensing No licensed content or personal data leaves Harvard-approved tools.
